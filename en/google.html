Google is one huge market.

Every day, thousands of people walk into this market looking for something. Google is the stallholder who points: "what you want is at that stall over there". Showing up on Google, what people call SEO, means getting that finger to point at you.

1

Every page on your site is a stall

Google doesn't show whole websites. It shows pages. When someone searches "boat tour in Paraty", it looks for the page that answers exactly that.

One page is one stall, in one aisle. People walking down that aisle see you. People looking for something else, in another aisle, never walk past.

  • Instagram only: you have no stall. Google rarely shows a social profile to someone looking for a service.
  • One page: one stall, showing up for your name and your main service.
  • One page per service: a stall in every aisle. Each one can show up for a different search.
  • Pages in another language: a stall in the aisle where foreign visitors search in their own language.

2

More pages only help if each one sells something different

Ten identical stalls side by side don't bring in more people. The stallholder sees they're all the same and hides the copies.

So I don't make pages just to fill space. Each page answers a question your customers really ask: how much it costs, how long it takes, what to bring, whether kids can come.

If your business does one thing

One good page does the job. It's not worth paying for pages that say the same thing in different words. I'll tell you that before you pick a package.

3

What makes the stallholder point at your stall

  • A clear sign: every page has a title that says exactly what it offers and where.
  • A full answer: the text answers the whole question, in plain words.
  • A fast stall: the site opens quickly on a phone, even on a weak connection.
  • The right address: the same name, address and phone on the site, on your Google profile and on social media.
  • A name badge: hidden code that tells Google what kind of business you are and where.
  • A good reputation: reviews on your Google profile, and the profile linked to the site.
4

What about AI? It asks the stallholder.

Lots of people now ask Google's AI or ChatGPT directly: "what's the best boat tour in Paraty?". The AI doesn't make the answer up. It mostly looks at two places: the Google business profile and the business's own website.

Without a website, you give the AI only half the information. A page that answers the question clearly gives you a better chance of being mentioned.
